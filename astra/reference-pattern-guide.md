## Causal analysis framework
- Approach: ordered displacement, prior confirmed extremes, local range position and elapsed time. Impulse alone is not a directional signal.
- Location: distinguish failure at a meaningful upper/lower area from an ordinary move within a range.
- Failure: a push cannot maintain acceptance; look for actual opposing response. Distinguish forming candles from closed bars and a single wick from structural failure.
- Failed recovery continuation: a rebound after a decline may fail below a meaningful high. This is distinct from reversal at a new high. Mirror for bullish continuation.
- Lower recovery: examine local higher lows after decline and evidence that selling fails to extend; distinguish temporary bounce.
- Entry quality: compare observable origin, current quote and opposing structure. Distinguish early proof from late extension; do not apply an invented universal distance threshold.
- Campaign: separate direction from exposure and broker fills. Multiple orders do not constitute multiple independent confirmations. Record weighted entry and observed basket behaviour, including adverse movement and incomplete exits.
- Outcomes: later footage is not causal evidence for an earlier decision. Never repair a missing entry prefix using displayed profit.

These hypotheses need independently verified tick sequences, negative examples and held-out replay. The clips do not establish complete trader rules, profitability or a verified prediction edge.
